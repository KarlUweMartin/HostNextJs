When I started at 3spin in 2018, the company was still structured largely like an agency. At first, I worked on short-term projects such as applications for trade fairs or presentations. Many of these projects involved playing 360° videos on VR headsets or tablets. Right from the start, I was able to demonstrate my experience with Unity3D by developing a cross-platform media player that was used across multiple projects at the same time.

After a few months at 3spin, I was assigned my first project as Lead Developer. It was an exploratory research project for a client called “HoloConference,” intended as a prototype for a conference software developed exclusively for the Microsoft HoloLens. In a HoloConference, users could meet as holograms, communicate with each other, and interact with objects in the room. Together with my team, we designed and implemented tools such as digital whiteboards, 3D pens, and virtual post-its.

The biggest challenge was transmitting a 3D scan of users in an acceptable amount of time while synchronizing the positions of all participants. Over roughly one year, we developed a working online prototype that was presented with great success at the DLD Conference in Munich. Due to complications during the COVID-19 pandemic, however, further development of HoloConference was put on hold indefinitely.

After the development of HoloConference was paused, I moved into the development team for 3spin DREAM. 3spin DREAM is a platform that allows users to create and manage VR training programs. The software runs on all common VR and AR headsets.

In the development team, we worked with Unity3D and the Microsoft Mixed Reality Toolkit to continuously improve the platform. In this role, I contributed as both a software developer and a UX designer. Since 3spin DREAM is used not only to run training programs but also as an editor, the team faced major usability challenges. All functions had to work reliably in both VR and AR environments.

As a UX designer, I designed various features and tools to make the creation of training programs more intuitive and efficient. I implemented many of these concepts in interactive prototypes, which were then tested with users to validate their suitability for official implementation. At the same time, I was also involved in the development of the official releases of 3spin DREAM and often took responsibility for the final implementation of these features.
